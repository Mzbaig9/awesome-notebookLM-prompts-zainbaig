RESPONSE TO THE ICNA SHARIAH COUNCIL

Re: Request for Clarification, Murabaha Financing Packet (Rev. 3.17.26), Stearns Bank National Association, Islamic Finance Division, and ICNA Relief

Attention: Shaykh Abdur Rahman Khan, Chairman, ICNA Shariah Council

As Salamu Alaykum wa Rahmatullahi wa Barakatuh.

We thank the Council for its careful review and for the four matters raised. We answer them in the order in which the Council has put them, and in each case we identify the provisions of the executed documentation on which the answer rests, so that the Council can verify the position against the papers rather than against our characterisation of them. Where the documentation does not yet carry the point as clearly as the Shariah requires, we say so plainly and propose the amendment that would cure it.

1. Ownership and Risk

The Bank acquires ownership of the Property through the Recipient acting as its appointed purchasing agent, and it acquires that ownership before, and not simultaneously with, its sale of the Property to the Recipient in its own capacity. The sequence contemplated by the documents is as follows. The Recipient first submits a Property Purchase Request identifying the Property, the Property Seller, the Original Price, the Total Acquisition Price, the Downpayment and the Agreed Profit. The Bank then issues the Agency Agreement, by which it appoints the Recipient "as our Agent to acquire the Property from the Property Seller for our account and benefit." The agent then acquires the Property from the Property Seller for the account of the Bank and with the Bank's funds. Only after that acquisition has been completed does the Recipient, now in its own capacity as buyer, submit the Property Purchase Offer, which opens with the confirmation that "we have acquired the Property interests, taken possession of the Property, and fulfilled our obligations as agent under the above referenced Agency Agreement." The Bank then issues the Property Purchase Acceptance, which conveys the Bank's interest and simultaneously terminates the agency. The Murabaha sale is concluded by that Offer and Acceptance and by nothing earlier.

The ownership so acquired by the Bank is beneficial and constructive rather than registered. The Agency Agreement provides that "the Agent will cause the deed and title to the Property to be transferred by the Property Seller directly to the Recipient and no deed or title shall be provided by the Property Seller to the Bank," and the Murabaha Agreement repeats under the heading Arrangement of Transaction; Deed that "the deed in respect of the Property will be made directly to and in the name of the Recipient from the Property Seller." We do not present this as anything other than what it is. Registered title never rests in the Bank's name. What the Bank acquires is the equitable and beneficial interest in the Property, acquired for its account by its agent with its funds, held for the interval between the Property Seller's conveyance and the Property Purchase Acceptance, and then conveyed to the Recipient. The reason for the arrangement is not Shariah but recordation and regulation, namely the avoidance of a double transfer tax, a double recording of title, and the bank regulatory difficulty of a national bank taking record title to real property. In the Hanafi position, and as reflected in the AAOIFI Shariah Standard on Murabaha to the Purchase Orderer, qabd may be hukmi, constructive, and is established by the passing of the object into the buyer's disposal and liability rather than by physical seizure or by any particular register.

On the risk of ownership, we accept without reservation the Council's premise that ownership without damaan is not ownership at all, and that a profit taken on an asset the seller never stood behind is the profit forbidden by the hadith of the Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, "wa la ribhu ma lam yudman," there is no lawful profit on that which one has not borne the liability for, reported by Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasai, and by the maxim al-kharaju bi al-damaan. The documents allocate risk as follows. Under the heading Passage of Ownership and Title; Risk of Loss; Liability, the Murabaha Agreement provides that "title to and risk of loss of all Property, including the interest of the Bank therein, shall vest in the Recipient in accordance with this Section immediately following Offer and Acceptance," and that "the Bank will acquire its interest in the Property on the Closing Date. Subsequently, the Recipient shall purchase and acquire all interests in the Property, including those of the Bank, on the Closing Date." The Bank's disclaimer of liability is expressly limited to loss or damage "occurring after delivery of the Purchase Acceptance." The general indemnity in the Murabaha Agreement has been amended, and the Council will see the amendment on the face of the marked packet, to confine the Recipient's indemnity in respect of events at the Property to those occurring "prior to the Closing or after Offer and Acceptance," so that the window during which the Bank holds the Property is carved out of the indemnity and left with the Bank. Insurance premiums and taxes attributable to the Property are included in the Total Acquisition Price and so are borne economically by the Bank as owner during its ownership window, and the Agency Agreement directs the agent to insure "on behalf of the Bank," that is, for the owner's account, not the agent's.

We will not overstate the interval. The Bank's ownership window is short, and in most closings it will be measured in minutes on a single Closing Date. Brevity does not defeat it. What would defeat it is the Bank standing outside the risk during that window, and we accept that two provisions still read more broadly than the Shariah allows. The first is the indemnity in the Agency Agreement, under which the agent indemnifies the Bank "from and against all third party Claims whatsoever and howsoever arising in relation to the Property and the acquisition of the Property by us," which on its face is not limited to the agent's negligence, breach or wilful misconduct and is not carved back for the Bank's own ownership window. The second is the indemnity in the Murabaha Agreement in respect of any lien on the Property and any "defect in the quality, quantity, or characteristics" of the Property, which, if read to cover defects existing at the time the Bank held the Property, would transfer to the buyer the very ayb liability that the seller must carry. We therefore propose, and the Bank is prepared to accept, that the Agency Agreement indemnity be qualified so that the agent is liable only for its own negligence, breach of the agency mandate or wilful misconduct, this being the settled rule that the wakeel is an ameen and not a daamin, and that the defect indemnity be expressed to apply only to matters arising after the Property Purchase Acceptance, leaving pre-Acceptance title defects and latent defects with the Bank as seller. With those two amendments the Bank's damaan during its ownership window is complete and unqualified, and we invite the Council to treat them as conditions of its opinion.

One further matter should be put before the Council rather than left for it to find. The Tax and Regulatory Matters Agreement states that for United States federal, state and local tax purposes and for bank regulatory purposes the Related Documents are to be treated as a single financing, that the Recipient is to be regarded at all times as the owner of the Property, that the Agreed Profit is to be reported as interest, and that any interest acquired by the Bank is acquired "solely for purposes of facilitating the Transaction." That agreement governs how the parties file and report, and it says so expressly. It does not govern the legal or the Shariah characterisation of the transfers, which are governed by the Murabaha Agreement and the Murabaha Documents, and the same agreement records that the Transaction "constitute[s] a murabaha compliant with the principles and precepts of Islamic Shariah." Different bodies of law characterise the same real transfers differently, and a tax characterisation does not unwind a sale that actually occurred. We raise it because the Council should have it in front of it, and because we would rather the Council weigh it now than discover it later.

2. Agency (Wakaalah)

Yes. A Wakaalah is contemplated and it is documented as a separate instrument, the Agency Agreement, executed before any disbursement is made and terminated before the Murabaha sale is completed.

Its terms are these. The Bank is the muwakkil and ICNA Relief, as Recipient, is the wakeel. The appointment is expressly limited, being "our agent only for the limited purpose of the purchase and acquisition of the Property from the Property Seller, the transfer of title to the Property, and the other matters described herein." The agent acquires "for our account and benefit," not for its own. The agent is unpaid, the Agency Agreement providing that the Bank shall not be liable "to pay any fee, cost, expense, or other remuneration of any type for the services rendered by the Agent," which is a valid wakaalah bi ghayr ajr. The agent may not materially amend, waive or cancel the purchase contract without the Bank's prior written consent. The Bank may terminate the agency at any time. The agency terminates automatically on the Property Purchase Acceptance, which states in terms that it "serves as evidence of the transfer and conveyance of all of our Property interests, and notice of termination of the Agency Agreement."

The two safeguards that matter most where the customer is also the purchasing agent are both present. First, the roles are kept sequential and are separately evidenced, so that the Recipient acts only as agent until it confirms completion of the agency in the Property Purchase Offer, and acts as buyer only thereafter. Second, the Recipient's promise to purchase is unilateral. The Property Purchase Request contains the Recipient's promise to acquire the Property once the Bank has acquired it, and no corresponding binding obligation is imposed on the Bank, which retains the discretion not to proceed. This is the muwaadah of one party only, binding on the promisor, which AAOIFI permits, and it is not the bilateral binding promise which we agree with the Council would render the arrangement a sale of what the seller does not own.

We record our own preference, which we have put to the Bank and now put to the Council. The Shariah position is materially stronger where the purchasing agent is a party other than the ultimate buyer, whether the Bank itself, an affiliate, a settlement agent or escrow agent, or the closing attorney. Where an independent agent is used, the Bank's ownership window is evidenced by a person with no interest in collapsing it, and the objection that the buyer bought from itself does not arise. We would ask the Council to consider recording in its opinion that the appointment of a third party purchasing agent is preferred wherever it is practicable, and that the Recipient may act as agent only where the two amendments proposed at paragraph 1 above are in place and the Offer and Acceptance are separately dated and executed on the closing record.

3. Murabaha Profit

The profit is fixed in a stated dollar amount and is irrevocably determined at the time the Murabaha contract is executed. It is not floating, it is not indexed, and it is not subject to repricing.

The Agreed Profit appears in the documents as a dollar figure, not as a rate. It is stated in the Property Purchase Request, restated in the Property Purchase Offer, and confirmed in the Property Purchase Acceptance. The Murabaha Price is defined as the Purchase Price plus the Agreed Profit plus the Downpayment, and the Murabaha Receivable, being the amount the Recipient owes, is the Purchase Price plus the Agreed Profit. The Murabaha Agreement fixes the moment at which the price becomes irrevocable, providing that "the Murabaha Receivable may be changed until the execution of the Property Purchase Offer and the Property Purchase Acceptance," which is to say that any adjustment must be made before the sale is concluded and none may be made afterwards. Consistently with that, all costs incurred by the Bank in respect of the Transaction are required to be added to the Purchase Price "prior to execution of the Property Purchase Offer and Property Purchase Acceptance," so that the total is complete and known when the parties contract. Prepayment carries no penalty and no termination fee, and any rebate for early settlement remains at the Bank's discretion rather than being a contractual entitlement of the Recipient, which is the position the Council will expect on ibra.

The Index Rate defined in the Murabaha Agreement, being the Wall Street Journal Prime Rate, is used only in the calculation of the Charity amount described in paragraph 4 below. It plays no part in the determination of the Agreed Profit and cannot alter the Murabaha Receivable. The Bank may, of course, have regard to prevailing market rates when it decides what margin to quote before contracting, as any seller has regard to the market when setting a price, and that use of a benchmark as a pricing reference at inception, exhausted the moment the price is agreed, is not the objectionable use.

Two points of confirmation should be given to the Council rather than assumed by it. First, the promissory Note and the payment schedule that will be executed with this Transaction must state a fixed obligation whose aggregate is exactly the Murabaha Receivable, and must not carry a variable rate. The specimen Standard Documents circulated with the packet are the Bank's conventional forms, one of which carries the legend that the note contains a variable interest rate. Those forms are amended by the Murabaha Agreement and Omnibus Amendment, which substitutes Agreed Profit for interest and Purchase Price for principal throughout, and which prevails over any conflicting provision, but we regard it as necessary rather than optional that the executed Note for this Transaction be a fixed obligation on its own face and not merely by operation of the omnibus amendment. Second, the Bank's right under the Additional Costs provision to recover increased costs arising from a Change in Law must not be capable of increasing the Murabaha Receivable. It is our position that it does not, since the Murabaha Receivable is fixed at Offer and Acceptance and such amounts fall within the Supplemental Charge, which the documents state "shall never form part of the Murabaha Receivable" and is "limited to actual costs incurred only." We are prepared to see that stated expressly.

4. Late Payment

The documentation does not permit the Bank to profit from delay. It provides for a Taazeer charge that is payable to charity and never to the Bank's income, for the recovery of actual costs, and for the ordinary security remedies.

The Charity provision operates as follows. On an Event of Default or Potential Default, including a failure to pay when due, the Bank may, but is not obliged to, give written notice assessing a Charity amount, which the Recipient then pays through the Bank to a charitable organisation or cause approved by the Bank's designated Shariah Advisory Board. The amount is one hundred dollars plus an amount equal to the Index Rate plus six per cent applied to the outstanding amount due, or the losses caused to the Bank by the default. The interpretive provisions of the Murabaha Agreement convert every reference in the Bank's standard forms to a "late fee," "late charge" or "default interest" into a reference to the Charity, so that no default interest survives anywhere in the documents. The Charity is defined so that it is separate from the Supplemental Charge and never forms part of the Murabaha Receivable, and the Murabaha Receivable itself cannot be increased by reason of delay. The Supplemental Charge, which covers the Bank's enforcement, collection, preservation and legal costs, is expressly "limited to actual costs incurred only."

That structure is the Taazeer and Tabarru mechanism the Council has in mind, and its Shariah basis is the ruling on the mumatil, the solvent debtor who withholds payment, of whom the Prophet, sallallahu alayhi wa sallam, said "matlu al ghaniyyi zulm," the procrastination of a solvent debtor is oppression, agreed upon, and "layyu al waajidi yuhillu irdahu wa uqubatah," the delay of one who has the means makes his honour and his punishment lawful, reported by Abu Dawud, al-Nasai and Ibn Majah. The charge is a deterrent and not a compensation for the time value of money, which is why it is stripped out of the Bank's revenue entirely.

We identify three refinements which we consider necessary for the mechanism to be sound in practice as well as in form, and which the Bank is prepared to accept.

The first concerns genuine hardship. The Charity provision as drafted does not distinguish between the solvent debtor who delays and the debtor who is genuinely unable to pay. The Shariah does distinguish between them, and the command in Surah al Baqarah, "and if the debtor is in difficulty, then grant him respite until a time of ease," is not discretionary as to its principle. We propose an express carve out providing that no Charity shall be assessed for any period during which the Recipient is unable to pay, as distinct from unwilling, and that the Bank shall grant reasonable respite in such a case. We would ask the Council to make this a condition of its opinion, and we note that the point has particular weight where the Recipient is a charitable organisation dependent on donation income.

The second concerns the trigger. The provision permits assessment on a Potential Default as well as on an actual failure to pay. Taazeer attaches to actual wrongful delay, not to a state of affairs that might become one. We propose that the Charity be assessable only on an actual failure to pay an amount when due, and that all other defaults be addressed through the Bank's other remedies rather than through a charitable charge.

The third concerns administration and evidence. We propose that the Charity be paid to an unaffiliated charitable organisation approved by the Shariah Advisory Board and not one which is an affiliate of the Bank or connected with it, that the Bank hold any such amounts in a segregated payable account and not recognise them as income for accounting purposes, that it remit them within a defined period, and that it provide the Recipient with evidence of onward payment on request. We also propose that the usury savings provision in the Murabaha Agreement, which directs that any excess of Agreed Profit or Charity over the maximum permitted by applicable law be applied to reduce the outstanding Murabaha Receivable, be amended so far as it concerns the Charity so that any such excess is simply not collected, since applying a charitable amount to reduce the buyer's debt converts a Tabarru into a benefit within the sale.

Beyond the Charity, the Bank's remedies on default are acceleration of the Murabaha Receivable, cash collateralisation of the outstanding amount, and enforcement against the Mortgage and the other security, together with the recovery of its actual costs as a Supplemental Charge. Acceleration is the calling in of a debt already fixed and does not increase it. The taking of a mortgage and of guarantees is rahn and kafaalah and is unobjectionable, and we note for the Council that the Recipient's obligation upon acceleration is the outstanding Murabaha Receivable and not the Murabaha Receivable increased by any charge referable to the passage of time.

Closing

To summarise, ownership passes to the Bank beneficially through its agent and is held with the risk of ownership for the interval before the Murabaha sale, subject to the two indemnity amendments we have proposed at paragraph 1, without which we would not ourselves regard the damaan as complete. A Wakaalah is used, it is separately documented, sequential and unpaid, and it terminates on the Acceptance, with our stated preference for an independent purchasing agent wherever practicable. The profit is a fixed dollar amount determined and made irrevocable at execution, with no repricing and no indexation, subject to confirmation that the executed Note is a fixed obligation on its face. Delay is addressed through a charitable Taazeer that never enters the Bank's income, together with actual cost recovery and the ordinary security remedies, subject to the three refinements at paragraph 4, of which the carve out for genuine inability to pay is the one we would press most strongly.

One matter which the Council did not raise but which we place before it for completeness is insurance. The Agency Agreement requires the Property to be fully insured on behalf of the Bank, with the cost included in the Total Acquisition Price. Where Takaful cover is commercially available for a property of this character it should be used, and where it is not, we have treated conventional cover as falling under the general affliction that presently attends commercial property in this market, which is a matter on which we would welcome the Council's own view.

We are ready to provide execution copies of any document, to answer any further question, and to attend a session of the Council in person or remotely at its convenience.

And Allah Knows Best.
