In the Name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.
As-salāmu ‘alaykum wa-rahmatullāhi wa-barakātuh.

Brother in Islam,

You mentioned that you had previously given your wife one ṭalāq and thereafter took her back. On a later occasion, you intended to tell her "I give you the second ṭalāq", however the words that came out of your mouth were "I give you 3 ṭalāqs". You have taken Allah as your witness that you did not intend three and that your intention was only the second ṭalāq.

In principle, a ṭalāq is looked at from two angles. The first is qaḍāʾ, which is the ruling that is passed on the apparent words that were spoken. This is how a Muslim judge would rule, since he can only judge by what was said and has no access to what was in the person's heart. The second is diyānah, which is the ruling between the person and Allah Taʿālā. Here, the person's true intention is taken into consideration, since Allah Taʿālā knows what was in his heart.[1]

When a husband intends to say one thing and his tongue slips to a word of ṭalāq that he did not intend, the ṭalāq takes effect in qaḍāʾ, because the word was clearly spoken. However, in diyānah, he is not held to what he did not intend.[2][3] The same applies to the number of ṭalāqs. If a husband intended a certain number and his tongue slipped to a greater number, then in qaḍāʾ the number that was spoken takes effect, and in diyānah only the number he intended takes effect. This is on condition that he is truthful and that he takes an oath on his intention.[4][5]

Accordingly, in qaḍāʾ, your words "I give you 3 ṭalāqs" would be taken as three ṭalāqs. However, in diyānah, between you and Allah Taʿālā, if your intention was only the second ṭalāq and the word "three" was a slip of the tongue, then only the second ṭalāq has taken effect.

In the enquired situation, you have given your wife a total of two ṭalāqs. Both are revocable (rajʿī), and you have the right to take your wife back during her ʿiddah without a new nikāḥ. You may do so by saying to her "I take you back", and it is preferable to make two people aware of it. If her ʿiddah has ended before you take her back, then a new nikāḥ with a new mahr will be required, with her consent.

It is important to note that this ruling is based on your truthfulness, and you will be answerable to Allah Taʿālā for it. If, in reality, you had intended three, then three ṭalāqs have taken effect and she is no longer permissible for you.

Allah knows best
Mufti Mirza-Zain Baig
